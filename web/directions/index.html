Problem 1 · most open

Threshold groups with consent

\(n\) members, \(m\) initiatives (acceptances unknown); initiative \(j\) forms only with at least \(k_j\) members.

An agent may ask its member at most \(b\) questions: “join \(j\) if it forms?”

Outcome: disjoint groups of members who said yes. Welfare: members placed.

New: groups with a minimum, not pairs; questions to failing groups are wasted.

Problem 2 · safest

A twin predicts, the person decides

An agent holds a prediction of its member (a “twin”), error unknown, and asks at most \(b\) questions.

The twin only picks whom to ask.

Goal: good if the twin is right, safe if not.

Why now: twins reach 83–86% of a person’s own consistency (Park et al. 2024).

Problems 3–5

Three more, from the same model

3

Grassroots vs central: separate them by load per agent, privacy, dropouts

4

Incentives of question order: agents exaggerate flexibility to be asked first

5

Value of pooling: correlated tastes save questions, but leak information

3 extends our single-option observation; 4 is the hardest; 5 is the formal “value of the Agora”.

App concept

“We need 2 more”: a card that recruits

Inside the app is Problem 1: whom to ask, in what order, under a cap of questions per person.

Direction

Start in a bounded community (a conference, a campus): density, safety, and a few hundred people suffice.
