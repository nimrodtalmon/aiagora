Who Goes First?

Conditional commitments in a group chat and in an agora

Nimrod Talmon · Ben-Gurion University · draft, November 21, 2026

Motivation

“I'll come if enough others come”

In a group chat someone must say “I'm in” first. If everyone waits, nothing happens.

Model

Conditions, groups, welfare

\(n\) members, one activity, several days. Member \(i\) has a private condition: the days it accepts, and a threshold \(\mathit{th}_i\): “I come if at least \(\mathit{th}_i\) others come.”

A group is a day and a set of members who all accept that day, has at least \(k\) members, and satisfies every member's threshold. At most one group per day; each member in at most one group.

Welfare: the number of members placed in groups.

Model

Two ways to form groups

Group chat: members read the chat one at a time, in any order, as often as they like. A member writes “I'm in” for an accepted day once at least \(\mathit{th}_i\) others are in for that day, and does not take it back. A day with at least \(k\) sign-ups forms a group.

Agora: agents hold their members' conditions and choose the groups with most members placed.

Theorem 4

One day: the chat stops early, the agora does not

With one day, for every order, the chat ends with the smallest \(c\) such that exactly \(c\) members have threshold at most \(c\).

The agora places the largest \(m \ge k\) such that at least \(m\) members have threshold at most \(m-1\).

The chat is Granovetter's cascade. Ten members “if 3”: chat 0, agora 10.

Thresholds 0, 2, 2, 3, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 9; \(k=4\).

Proposition 5

Several days: the best groups are hard to find

Deciding whether all members can be placed is NP-hard, even if every threshold is 2.

From exact cover by 3-sets: days are the sets and members the elements, each accepting the days of its sets; a group of at least three on a day is then exactly that set.

In practice, an integer program finds the best groups for neighborhoods of 400 members in under a second.

Simulation

Few eager members: the chat forms little

100 members, 10 activities from popular to niche, 7 days, \(k=4\); thresholds 2 to 6, except eager members (threshold 0).

With 10% eager: the chat places 13% of the interested; the agora, 69%.

Chat: random reading order. Agora: the best groups (integer program). 20 neighborhoods per point.

Simulation

The gap stays as the neighborhood grows

10% eager members; neighborhoods from 25 to 400 members.

At 400 members: the chat places 53%, the agora 96%.

Same setting and methods as the previous slide.

Related work

Closest work, and the difference

Threshold models

Granovetter 1978 (AJS)

each joins once enough others did; the cascade stops early

Assurance contracts

Tabarrok 1998 (Public Choice)

one common threshold, run by an entrepreneur

Critical mass

Oliver & Marwell 1988 (ASR)

the few interested must “find one another”

Group activity selection

Darmann et al. 2012 (WINE)

a planner knows all; our problem is a special case

New here: personal thresholds and days, and the gap between a public cascade and agents that choose the groups.
