The Scale Paradox

Bigger group chats form fewer groups, unless agents route

Nimrod Talmon · Ben-Gurion University · draft, November 18, 2026

Model

Initiatives that need \(k\) members

\(n\) members. For each type \(t\), \(a_t\) members accept it (the set \(A_t\)); one is its initiator.

The group forms if at least \(k\) members of \(A_t\) are reached; a reached non-member of \(A_t\) is bothered.

Gain \(B\) if it forms; cost \(c\) per bothered. Knowing \(a_t\), the initiator announces iff, in the worst case, it forms and \(B \ge c \cdot \text{bothered}\).

Welfare: groups formed. The cost \(c\) is social: fear of spamming.

Model

Two channels, and a growing community

Broadcast: an announcement reaches all other \(n-1\) members.

Agora: agents guess whether their members accept \(t\); at most \(\mathit{err}\) guesses per type are wrong. The initiator's agent invites members guessed to accept, in any order, until \(k-1\) accept.

Growing community: type \(t\) has a share \(s_t\); with \(n\) members, \(a_t = \lfloor s_t n \rfloor\).

Orange: accepts. In the agora, two guesses are wrong.

Theorem 1

Broadcast: each type forms only in a window

In broadcast, type \(t\) forms iff \(a_t \ge k\) and \(n - a_t \le \tfrac{B}{c}\).

So a type with share \(s\) forms only for \(n\) in an interval inside \(\big[\tfrac{k}{s}, \tfrac{B}{c(1-s)}\big]\).

If \(s < \tfrac{k}{k+B/c}\), it never forms.

It bothers exactly \(n - a_t\) members; both conditions are monotone.

Theorem 2

Agora: once a type forms, it keeps forming

In the agora, type \(t\) forms iff \(a_t \ge k + \mathit{err}\) and \(c \cdot \min(\mathit{err},\, n - a_t) \le B\).

So if \(\mathit{err} \le \tfrac{B}{c}\), a type with share \(s\) forms for every \(n \ge \tfrac{k+\mathit{err}}{s}\).

Always \(k-1\) acceptors invited, at most \(\mathit{err}\) members bothered; both bounds tight.

Example

Broadcast peaks and dies; the agora grows

300 types with shares \(s_t = \tfrac{0.5}{t}\), and \(k=4\), \(\tfrac{B}{c} = 150\).

Broadcast: 19 types ever form; at most 19 groups (152–154 members); none above 300.

Agora: all 300 from 2,400 members; with \(\mathit{err} = 10\), from 8,400.

Exact counts. Theorems checked by brute force: 1,215 small cases.

Related work

Closest work, and the difference

Attention as a commons

Anderson & de Palma 2009 (RAND)

senders over-use readers’ attention; no groups, no size result

Niche crowding

Falkinger 2005 (IZA working paper)

larger reach raises the bar to be noticed; no groups

Critical mass

Oliver & Marwell 1988 (ASR)

larger groups reach k; the few must “find one another” — in words

Group activity selection

Darmann et al. 2018 (IJGT)

groups with lower size bounds; finding members is free

New here: threshold groups found through one channel whose cost falls on all members; the number of groups as a function of community size.
